Compliance
Risk
Compliance is identified as a risk 
due to the frequent changes in 
regulatory requirements, making 
it challenging to consistently 
maintain adherence across all 
applicable laws. Operating in the 
financial sector, the Company 
manages large volumes of 
sensitive client information, and 
any lapses — whether in data 
protection, operational processes, 
or reporting — could result in 
regulatory breaches, penalties, and 
reputational harm.
The Company undertakes regular 
reviews of applicable compliance 
requirements and actively monitors 
regulatory updates to ensure the timely 
implementation of necessary changes. 
We have a software in place (“Velox”) 
wherein every employee has to submit 
the details of their investments/holdings 
and dealings, etc., including prior 
approval for trading. The regulatory 
requirements, as given by SEBI, are 
built within the software. To further 
strengthen compliance management, 
periodic training is provided to 
employees, emphasising the 
importance of regulatory adherence 
and raising awareness of the 
consequences of non-compliance.
Negative: 
Failure to comply 
with regulatory 
requirements can lead 
to financial penalties, 
legal liabilities, and 
increased operational 
expenses associated with 
remediation efforts.
2
Data 
Protection & 
Information 
Security
Risk
The Company’s reliance on 
digital infrastructure for managing 
transactions, customer data, 
and internal operations makes 
data protection and information 
security a critical area of risk. 
With the increasing frequency and 
sophistication of cyber threats—such 
as phishing, ransomware, and third-
party breaches—the potential for 
data compromise poses significant 
operational, reputational, and 
financial consequences.
The enactment of regulations 
such as the Digital Personal Data 
Protection Act (DPDPA), 2023 has 
further elevated the importance 
of responsible data governance, 
placing legal obligations on the 
Company to protect personal 
data and ensure privacy rights of 
individuals. Any failure to comply 
with DPDPA or effectively secure 
sensitive information could result 
in penalties, legal liabilities, and 
erosion of investor trust.
Protecting customer confidentiality, 
ensuring business continuity, and 
maintaining investor confidence are 
central to the Company’s long-term 
credibility. 
To mitigate data protection and 
information security risks, the Company 
has established a comprehensive 
governance framework, anchored by 
its “Policy on Privacy and Information 
Security,” which defines the rights and 
obligations related to the use and 
protection of personally identifiable 
and confidential investor information.
Our organisation has implemented 
a robust cybersecurity management 
framework based on international 
standards such as ISO/IEC 27001 
and NIST Cybersecurity Framework, 
and in line with SEBI circular related 
to Cyber Security and Cyber Resilience 
Framework. A dedicated Data Leak 
Policy, regular Vulnerability Assessment 
and Penetration Testing (VAPT), Red 
Teaming, and half yearly Cyber Audit 
further strengthen the Company’s 
defences.
We have implemented several 
initiatives: Data Leak Protection 
solution, Continuous Automated 
Red Teaming , Breach and Attack 
Simulation and Attack Surface 
Monitoring  solution, Endpoint 
Detection and Response solutions, 
Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI) - Brand, 
Domain and Dark Web Monitoring , 
and Software Bill of Materials (SBOM). 
In parallel, employee awareness is 
actively enhanced through quarterly 
phishing simulations and regular 
communication campaigns to 
address evolving cyber threats. These 
measures collectively ensure proactive 
risk management, compliance with 
regulatory frameworks and protection 
of stakeholders’ interests.
Negative: 
Failure to adequately 
protect personal and 
sensitive data can lead 
to significant financial 
consequences for the 
Company, including 
regulatory penalties 
under the DPDPA 
(2023), litigation costs, 
and compensation 
claims from affected 
stakeholders.
Data breaches or cyber 
incidents can also 
result in operational 
disruptions, loss of 
investor confidence, and 
reputational damage—
potentially impacting 
client retention and 
future inflows. In 
the long term, weak 
information security 
can undermine the 
Company’s credibility, 
adversely affecting its 
brand value and market 
competitiveness.

a secure work environment and enhancing productivity. Our health and safety management system is built on four key pillars:
	
	
Compliance: UTI AMC strictly adheres to statutory health and safety regulations. Physical security measures include 
annual evacuation drills, fire safety briefings, hands-on training in the use of firefighting equipment, routine inspections and 
refilling of fire extinguishers, and monthly checks of detectors and sensors.
	
	
Risk Management: The Company regularly identifies, assesses, and mitigates OHS-related hazards and risks to minimise

Training: Employees receive comprehensive OHS training, including orientation, fire safety procedures, and on-the-job

What are the processes used to identify work-related hazards and assess risks on a routine and non-
routine basis by the entity?
	
Due to the nature of the business, work-related hazards are minimal.  Nevertheless, the Company maintains a proactive 
approach to identifying and mitigating both routine and non-routine risks with employee well-being at the core. Processes 
followed include: 
	
-	
Health Risk Assessments: Conducted in partnership with a healthcare service provider, these cover physical mobility, 
body pain impact, emotional well-being, fatigue, and energy levels, helping to identify potential occupational health 
concerns and areas of intervention.
	
-	
Routine Workplace Interventions: Regular walkthroughs and visual assessments are conducted to monitor equipment, 
work practices, and environmental conditions for any emerging risks. 
	
-	
Non-Routine Risk Monitoring: E-Surveillance and ad-hoc audits are carried out to identify less frequent but critical 
hazards such as fire, electrical faults, or security breaches.
	
-	
Emergency Preparedness: Regular fire drills, safety advisories, and emergency response training ensure readiness. 
Security personnel are trained in fire safety protocols and equipment use. 
	
-	
Employee Awareness: Safety-related advisories are shared periodically to enhance risk awareness and promote a 
culture of safety. 
	
While complete elimination of risk may not always be possible, UTI AMC remains committed to taking all reasonably practical 
measures to ensure a secure and healthy work environment for all employees.
